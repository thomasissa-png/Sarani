## 0. Context & Rationale

Sarani has grown 100% by referral to date. LinkedIn is the first proactive acquisition channel — the shift from passive word-of-mouth to systematic inbound. The existing LinkedIn page (~1,000 followers) is a latent asset: the client roster (TikTok, Sony, Adidas, GEODIS, L'Oréal) is more compelling than 95% of the content published by competing agencies. The strategy below is built around one principle: **the work speaks, Sarani amplifies it.**

Budget constraint is real: 500€/month total acquisition. Organic LinkedIn must carry the primary load. Paid acts as a precision amplifier on posts that already demonstrate organic pull — not a replacement for content quality.

This is a **B2B-first strategy.** Platform: LinkedIn only (Phase 2). Instagram and other channels are out of scope for the 90-day sprint.

## 3. Content Pillars

Five pillars. Each post maps to exactly one. The ratio below is mandatory — deviation signals brand drift.

### Pillar 1 — Proof Points (35% of posts)
**What it is:** Named client + real numbers + specific result. The hardest-working pillar. Sophie evaluates every agency on "who else do you work with?" This pillar answers that question before she asks it.

**Examples:**
- "Sony needed Black Friday banners the same morning. We delivered. 155€ per banner."
- "1,500+ video edits a month for TikTok. Every month."
- "GEODIS: 350 presentations in 3 weeks. 5,700 slides. 8,500€."

**Format:** Single image with stat overlay, or short-form text post with visual. Never a wall of text. The number is the hook.

**Voice check:** Evidence-first. One anchor fact per post. Named client mandatory.

---

### Pillar 2 — Behind the Process (25% of posts)
**What it is:** How Sarani delivers what it promises. The 5-continent relay. The 24/7 workflow. The briefing system. The revision process. This pillar builds credibility for the proof points — it answers "how is that even possible?"

**Examples:**
- The time-zone relay: how a brief sent at 11pm Paris time is in production by 7am
- How the 18-language team is structured (not a translation agency — native speakers in market)
- The briefing format that makes D+1 possible (what Sarani needs from a client to deliver in 24h)

**Format:** Carousel (best for process explanation), or native LinkedIn article for depth. Text-only threads for conversational takes.

**Voice check:** Direct. Explain the mechanism, not the aspiration. "Here is how" not "we believe in."

---

### Pillar 3 — Thought Leadership (20% of posts)
**What it is:** Sarani's view on enterprise creative production — the industry, the dysfunction, the shifts. This pillar positions the agency as a peer to Sophie, not a vendor. It earns organic reach because it sparks discussion.

**Examples:**
- "Why every big agency pitch takes 6 weeks and the first asset takes 3 more"
- "The hidden cost of revision caps (it's not 200€ per round — it's the delay that kills campaigns)"
- "What TikTok taught us about creative velocity at scale"
- "The enterprise creative bottleneck: why your agency always says two weeks"

**Format:** Text-only post (highest reach for thought leadership on LinkedIn), or short carousel for structured arguments.

**Voice check:** Assured. Opinion-driven. Never hedge. Never "we think it might be." State the position.

---

### Pillar 4 — Team & Culture (10% of posts)
**What it is:** The humans behind the 35-expert network. Faces, names, locations, stories. This pillar makes the 5-continent claim tangible. Sophie needs to trust that a real team is on the other side of the brief.

**Examples:**
- A designer in Nairobi who handles the overnight shift for European clients
- The 18-language fact: a post introducing the specific languages and the team members behind them
- "Our day starts in Auckland and ends in Los Angeles"

**Format:** Photo + short text. Authentic, not produced. No stock photography.

**Voice check:** Warm. Human first. Numbers second (here, names and faces outrank metrics).

---

### Pillar 5 — Invitations to Brief (10% of posts)
**What it is:** Direct, low-friction prompts that translate content engagement into contact. Not hard-sell. Not "contact us." A specific scenario that makes Sophie say "this is exactly my problem."

**Examples:**
- "If you have a campaign live by Friday and you don't have the assets yet — send us a brief now."
- "Black Friday is in [X weeks]. We've done it for Sony. We can do it for you."
- "Running assets across 5 markets and one language team is slowing you down. We have 18."

**Format:** Short text post. One CTA. Always links to contact form or direct message. Never a landing page without a clear next step.

**Voice check:** Direct. No "don't hesitate." One instruction. Present tense.

## 4. Content Ratio & Weekly Cadence

**Target frequency:** 4 posts per week (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday)
**Realistic minimum:** 3 posts per week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday)

Week structure:
- **Monday:** Proof Point — starts the week with evidence. Sophie checks LinkedIn Monday morning.
- **Tuesday/Wednesday:** Behind the Process or Thought Leadership — mid-week depth content.
- **Thursday:** Thought Leadership or Team & Culture — conversation-driving post before weekend.
- **Friday:** Invitation to Brief or Proof Point — end-of-week with a clear CTA (decisions happen Friday before a new week starts).

**Ratio enforcement (per 4-week block):**
- Proof Points: 6–7 posts
- Behind the Process: 4–5 posts
- Thought Leadership: 3–4 posts
- Team & Culture: 1–2 posts
- Invitations to Brief: 1–2 posts

## 5. Format Recommendations

| Format | Best use | Target frequency | LinkedIn benchmark |
|--------|----------|------------------|--------------------|
| **Single image (stat overlay)** | Proof Points — a number over a visual | 2×/week | High reach on company pages |
| **Native text post (no image)** | Thought Leadership, Invitations to Brief | 1×/week | Best organic reach for algorithmic distribution |
| **Carousel (multi-image)** | Behind the Process, structured case studies | 1×/week | Highest engagement rate: 6.60% avg (2026 benchmark) |
| **Short video (60–90 sec)** | Behind the Process, team stories | 1× per 2 weeks | 5× more engagement than static; 5.60% avg engagement |
| **Poll** | Thought Leadership (to spark discussion) | 1× per month max | Relevant only above 50K followers for impressions; use sparingly |
| **LinkedIn Article** | Deep-dive process or thought leadership | 1× per month | Long-term SEO and credibility signal, not short-term reach |

**Format NOT to use:**
- Infographics with dense text — they compress poorly on mobile
- Repurposed Instagram content — format mismatch kills reach
- Auto-published content without a human engagement check in the first 30 minutes post-publish

## 6. Voice on LinkedIn

Governed entirely by `docs/copy/brand-voice.md`. Key calibrations for the LinkedIn context:

**Opening line rules (the hook):**
- First line must work as a standalone scroll-stopper. LinkedIn truncates after 2–3 lines.
- Lead with a number, a client name, or a specific scenario — never with "We are excited to share."
- Correct: "TikTok sends us 300–500 videos a week. We return 1,500+ edits a month."
- Incorrect: "We're proud to announce that we've been working with TikTok on an exciting campaign."

**Post length:**
- Proof Points: 3–6 lines of body text. Short. The number carries the post.
- Behind the Process: 6–12 lines. Structured. Short sentences. White space between paragraphs.
- Thought Leadership: 8–15 lines. Argument → evidence → conclusion. No walls of text.
- Invitations to Brief: 2–4 lines max. Hook + one CTA.

**Emoji policy:** None. Consistent with brand-voice.md. Confidence does not need visual decoration.

**Hashtag policy:** See Section 7.

## 8. Amplification Strategy (500€/month)

### Organic-first rule
No paid amplification on a post that has not demonstrated organic engagement. If a post gets <10 reactions in 24 hours, it is not ready to amplify — diagnose the hook, not the budget.

### Budget allocation (monthly)
| Tactic | Budget | Objective | Format |
|--------|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Post boosting — top organic posts** | 200€ | Extend reach of best-performing Proof Point or Thought Leadership post(s) to cold audiences matching Sophie's profile | Boosted post |
| **Thought Leader Ads — Thomas (founder)** | 150€ | Amplify founder content to warm retargeting pool (website visitors, engaged LinkedIn users) | Thought Leader Ad |
| **Lead Gen Form — single always-on unit** | 150€ | Generate direct qualified leads from cold audiences | Lead Gen Form Ad |

### Audience targeting for paid
**Cold (awareness):**
- Job title: Head of Marketing, Marketing Director, VP Marketing, VP Brand, Content Director
- Company size: 5,000+ employees
- Industries: Consumer goods, logistics, technology, luxury, retail
- Geography: France, UK, UAE, Netherlands, Germany, Singapore

**Warm (retargeting):**
- Website visitors (tracked via Umami — build pixel equivalent once live)
- LinkedIn page engagers (last 180 days)
- Video viewers (50%+)

### Activation timeline
- **Week 6–7:** Organic-only. No paid. Build baseline and identify top-performing posts.
- **Week 8:** Activate 500€/month paid budget on best organic posts. Set initial performance baseline.
- **Week 10+:** Optimize based on CPL data. Shift budget toward highest-converting format.

## 9. KPIs & Performance Thresholds

### Post-level metrics (evaluate per post, 72h after publish)
| Metric | Minimum acceptable | Target | Action if below minimum |
|--------|-------------------|--------|------------------------|
| Engagement rate | 1.5% | 3%+ | Audit hook. Rewrite opening line on next post. |
| Impressions | 500 | 2,000+ | Check posting time. Amplify manually (comment from founder account). |
| Comments | 2 | 5+ | Post may be too passive. Add a question at the end. |
| Saves | 1 | 5+ | Directional for quality content. Track over time. |

### Account-level metrics (evaluate monthly)
| Metric | Month 1 target | Month 2 target | Month 3 target |
|--------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Net new followers | +50 | +75 | +100 |
| Total organic impressions | 8,000 | 15,000 | 30,000 |
| Average post engagement rate | 2% | 2.5% | 3%+ |
| Profile visits | 200 | 400 | 800 |
| LinkedIn-attributed form submissions | 1 | 2–3 | 3–5 |

### Benchmark context (2026 LinkedIn data)
- Average B2B company engagement rate: 1.8–2.2%
- Top-quartile B2B company engagement rate: 4%+
- Carousel average engagement: 6.60%
- Video average engagement: 5.60%
- Pages posting 3–5×/week see 2× engagement vs. inconsistent posters

Sarani's target of 3%+ engagement rate is above average but achievable given the named-client proof points — the content has structural advantages that average B2B accounts do not have.
