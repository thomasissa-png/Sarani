## 1. Voice Attributes

Sarani's voice rests on four attributes. Each one has a precise definition, concrete calibration, and examples of correct and incorrect application. These are not aspirational adjectives. They are operational standards.

---

### 1.1 Assured

**Definition:** Sarani speaks from a track record, not from aspiration. Every claim is backed by a named client, a real number, or a verifiable result. Sarani never hedges, never over-promises, and never uses vague superlatives. Confidence comes from the evidence — not from the volume of the claim.

**Calibration:**
```
Tentative ◆─────────────────── Arrogant
              ▲ Sarani sits here
     (3/10 tentative, 7/10 confident — never reaches arrogant)
```

**We sound like this:**
- "Sony's Black Friday banners. Ordered in the morning. Delivered the same day."
- "TikTok trusted us with 1,500+ video edits a month. Every month."
- "GEODIS needed 350 presentations rebranded. We delivered 5,700 slides in 3 weeks."

**We do not sound like this:**
- "We believe we can be your best creative partner."
- "Our world-class team consistently delivers outstanding results."
- "We think you'll really love working with us."

**The rule:** Never praise yourself. Point at the work. If the work is impressive, the reader draws their own conclusion.

---

### 1.2 Direct

**Definition:** No agency jargon. No filler. No sentences that could be cut without losing meaning. Sarani's primary reader — Sophie, Head of Marketing at a global enterprise — reads copy while managing three simultaneous campaigns. She has zero patience for hedged language, overlong sentences, or corporate phrasing. Every sentence must earn its place in under two seconds.

**Calibration:**
```
Corporate ◆─────────────────── Blunt
                       ▲ Sarani sits here
     (8/10 direct, never reaches blunt or rude)
```

**We sound like this:**
- "A banner in 24 hours. Starts at 155€."
- "Unlimited revisions. Fixed prices. Zero surprises."
- "No retainer. No minimum commitment. Pay per project."

**We do not sound like this:**
- "We offer competitive, industry-leading turnaround times with flexible pricing options tailored to your specific needs."
- "Our holistic approach to creative solutions ensures synergies across all touchpoints."
- "We are committed to delivering best-in-class results through our innovative process."

**The rule:** Maximum 20 words for a claim. Maximum 30 words for an explanation. If a sentence is over 30 words, split it. If a word adds nothing, delete it.

---

### 1.3 Warm

**Definition:** Sarani is a partner, not a vendor. The tone is the colleague who always delivers — professional, human, never cold. Sophie has been burned by agencies before. Warmth here is not cheerfulness. It is reliability that feels personal. The trust of "I know you'll come through" rather than the formality of "as per your request."

**Calibration:**
```
Cold/Corporate ◆─────────────────── Informal/Casual
                    ▲ Sarani sits here
     (5/10 — professional warmth, not startup friendliness)
```

**We sound like this:**
- "We're the team your team calls when everything has to be right by tomorrow."
- "Send us a brief at 11pm. You'll have a first version by 9am."
- "Not satisfied with your first project? No invoice. Simple as that."

**We do not sound like this:**
- "As a leading creative solutions provider, we are committed to delivering value."
- "Thank you for your inquiry. A member of our team will be in touch within 3–5 business days."
- "We look forward to the opportunity to potentially collaborate with your organization."

**The rule:** Write as if you are the person Sophie calls directly. Human voice. Present tense. Active verb. No passive bureaucracy.

---

### 1.4 Evidence-first

**Definition:** Every significant claim must be anchored to a named entity or a number. This is the structural habit that makes the other three attributes possible. Assured requires evidence. Direct requires specificity. Warm requires credibility. Named clients and real numbers provide all three simultaneously.

**Calibration:**
```
Vague ◆─────────────────── Over-referenced
                  ▲ Sarani sits here
     (One anchor fact per claim — not zero, not five)
```

**We sound like this:**
- "Up to 60% savings vs traditional agencies." (number)
- "35 experts, 5 continents, 18 languages." (three stacked numbers)
- "L'Oréal, Pernod Ricard, TikTok — these clients set the quality bar." (names)

**We do not sound like this:**
- "Significant savings compared to traditional agencies."
- "A large, diverse team working globally."
- "We work with some of the world's biggest brands."

**The rule:** Numbers beat adjectives. Named clients beat "leading brands." If you cannot name a number or a client, you are not ready to make the claim.

## 2. Tone Matrix

The voice (Assured, Direct, Warm, Evidence-first) is constant. The tone — the emotional register applied to the voice — shifts by context. This matrix defines the correct shift for every Sarani touchpoint.

| Context | Tone shift | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Homepage hero** | Maximum confidence. Lead with D+1 and a named client. Sophie has 3 seconds to decide if this is worth her time. No warmth preamble — proof first. | "Enterprise-quality creative. Delivered in 24 hours. TikTok, Sony, Adidas trust us with theirs." |
| **Case study** | Narrative confidence. Tell the problem, the scale, the result. Let the numbers do the selling. Sophie reads case studies to validate — give her the validation she is looking for. | "GEODIS needed 350 presentations rebranded in 3 weeks. We delivered 5,700 slides. 8,500€. Done." |
| **Pricing page** | Clinical transparency. No softening, no excuses. Marc reads this page. Fixed numbers, clear scope, no asterisks. Warmth comes through the guarantee, not through adjectives. | "Simple banner: 155–470€. Full rebranding: 5,000€. First project satisfaction or no invoice." |
| **Contact form** | Friction-minimizing, warm encouragement. Sophie is about to commit to a brief. Remove any obstacle. One CTA, no complexity, a human promise. | "Tell us what you need. We'll get back to you within the hour." |
| **Success message (form submitted)** | Warmth + speed signal. Sophie just sent the brief. Her anxiety: will they respond fast? Immediately confirm the speed commitment. | "Got it. Expect a response within the hour — usually faster." |
| **Error message** | Direct, non-apologetic, immediately actionable. Sophie has no patience for vague errors. Tell her what went wrong, tell her what to do. One sentence each. | "That didn't go through. Try again or email us directly: hello@sarani.studio" |
| **LinkedIn post** | Peer-to-peer, case-study driven. Sophie reads LinkedIn on her commute. Write as if a colleague is sharing a result, not as a brand broadcasting. Lead with a number or a specific scene. Never sell in the first sentence. | "TikTok needed 300 videos edited in one week. We delivered 340. Here's how we structured it." |
| **Email outreach** | Direct, short, one ask. Cold email to a Head of Marketing. Sentence 1: relevant proof. Sentence 2: the specific proposition. Sentence 3: one question or CTA. Nothing else. | "We edit 1,500 videos a month for TikTok's content team. If your production pipeline ever needs overflow capacity — we can start this week. Worth a 15-minute call?" |
| **Onboarding (first project confirmation)** | Warm + operational. Sophie just said yes. Confirm the timeline, confirm the contact, confirm the guarantee. She needs to feel she made a safe choice. | "You're in. We'll have a first version ready by tomorrow, [DATE]. One contact, no handoffs: [NAME]. If it's not right, we iterate — no extra charge." |
| **Upsell (suggesting additional services)** | Evidence-based suggestion, not a sales push. Anchor to work already delivered. Let the track record do the asking. | "We handled your banners in 24h. We do the same for video. Want to see what we did for Sony's TV launch?" |

## 3. Writing Rules

These rules apply to every piece of Sarani copy — website, email, LinkedIn, UI text, documentation. No exceptions.

### 3.1 Sentence length
- **Claims:** 20 words maximum. If it is over 20 words, split it or cut it.
- **Explanations:** 30 words maximum. If it is over 30 words, it is two sentences, not one.
- **Body copy:** Vary rhythm. Short-short-medium. Never three long sentences in a row.

### 3.2 Always say (required vocabulary)
| Use this | In this context |
|----------|----------------|
| "D+1 delivery" or "in 24 hours" | Any delivery speed claim |
| "Fixed prices" | Any pricing mention |
| "Unlimited revisions" | Any revision policy mention |
| "First project satisfaction or no invoice" | Any guarantee context — always verbatim |
| "35 experts, 5 continents, 18 languages, 24/7" | Any team/scale mention — verbatim as a unit |
| Named client + deliverable + result | All quality claims (e.g., "Sony, 125 assets, TV launch") |
| "Up to 60% savings vs traditional agencies" | All cost comparison contexts |
| "No retainer. No minimum commitment." | All subscription-model comparison contexts |
| "Enterprise-quality" | When qualifying the service level |
| "Start a project" | Primary CTA — preferred over "Contact us" or "Get in touch" |

### 3.3 Never say (banned vocabulary)
| Banned term | Why | Replace with |
|-------------|-----|-------------|
| "Cheap" / "affordable" | Implies low quality. Sarani is premium-priced for value. | "Fixed pricing," "transparent rates," "up to 60% savings" |
| "World-class" / "best-in-class" | Vague, unverifiable — every agency says this | Name TikTok, Sony, Adidas |
| "Creative solutions" | Agency jargon, meaningless | Name the actual deliverable (banner, video, deck) |
| "Holistic" | Corporate filler | Delete and start fresh |
| "Synergies" | Corporate filler | Delete and start fresh |
| "Innovative" (without proof) | Overused, meaningless without evidence | "We edited 1,500 TikTok videos in a month" |
| "Passionate about" | Cliché | Show the work, not the feeling |
| "Boutique agency" | Implies small, precious, slow | "International creative agency" |
| "Full-service" (alone) | Means nothing without specifics | List the actual four pillars |
| "Disruptive" | Startup cliché — Sarani has enterprise clients | Not appropriate |
| "Competitive rates" | Vague hedge | Publish the actual rate |
| "Tailored to your needs" | Filler phrase | Describe what is actually tailored |
| "We believe / we think" | Hedges the claim | State the claim directly |
| "Don't hesitate to" | Passive, dated | "Send us a brief" / "Start a project" |

### 3.4 Punctuation and capitalisation
- **Em dashes (—):** Use to connect a claim to its proof. "Sony's Black Friday banners — delivered the same day." Preferred over parentheses in marketing copy.
- **Sentence fragments:** Allowed and encouraged for rhythm in hero sections and CTAs. "24 hours. No surprises. No invoice if it's not right."
- **Capitalisation:** Sentence case for all UI labels and body copy. Title case only for proper nouns (Sarani, TikTok, Sony) and formal product names. NEVER full-caps outside of logo usage.
- **Exclamation marks:** Banned. Confidence does not need exclamation marks.
- **Ellipsis:** Banned in marketing copy. It implies hesitation. Use a full stop.

### 3.5 Numbers and proof
- Always use numerals for numbers, not words. "35 experts" not "thirty-five experts."
- Currency: "155€" not "€155" (European convention, consistent with deck).
- Large numbers: "1,500+" not "over one thousand five hundred."
- Percentages: "60%" not "sixty percent."
- Always pair a number with context: "350 presentations in 3 weeks" not just "350 presentations."
- Named clients always outrank unnamed numbers. "Sony trusted us" > "A global electronics brand trusted us."

### 3.6 Reader address
- **Website copy (Sophie):** "you" and "your" — direct address, second person singular. Not "clients," not "partners."
- **Case studies:** Third person for the client ("GEODIS needed..."), first person plural for Sarani ("We delivered...").
- **LinkedIn:** First person plural ("We...") for Sarani team posts. Second person ("You...") for posts framed as a question or a challenge.
- **Email outreach:** Second person singular ("you," "your team") — always personal, never "Dear Sir/Madam."
- **Error/success messages:** Second person ("Got it." is addressed to the user implicitly). Avoid "the user" or "the client."
- **Never use:** "one" as a pronoun (too formal), "they" for a specific named client (name them instead).

## 5. Headline Formulas

Seven proven formulas calibrated to Sarani's positioning and Sophie's decision criteria. Each formula has a definition, the structural pattern, and a live Sarani example ready to use.

---

### Formula 1 — Client + Volume + Frequency
**When to use:** Hero sections, case study titles, LinkedIn opening lines.
**Pattern:** `[Named client] trusted us with [volume] [deliverable] [time unit].`
**Example:**
> TikTok trusted us with 1,500+ video edits a month.

---

### Formula 2 — Problem → Result (no agency fluff)
**When to use:** Case study headlines, homepage section intros, cold email subject lines.
**Pattern:** `[Client] needed [problem]. We delivered [specific result].`
**Example:**
> GEODIS needed 350 presentations rebranded in 3 weeks. We delivered 5,700 slides.

---

### Formula 3 — The Speed Proof
**When to use:** Hero subheads, feature highlights, LinkedIn posts about turnaround.
**Pattern:** `[Deliverable], [delivered timeframe]. [Named client or price anchor].`
**Example:**
> Black Friday banners, delivered the same day. 155€ per banner.

---

### Formula 4 — The Unlimited Stack
**When to use:** Value proposition sections, pricing page intros, objection-handling blocks.
**Pattern:** `[Value 1]. [Value 2]. [Value 3].` (three short stacks, no connectors)
**Example:**
> Unlimited revisions. Fixed prices. Zero surprises.

---

### Formula 5 — The Alternative Reframe
**When to use:** Positioning headlines, competitive comparison sections, "Why Sarani" sections.
**Pattern:** `The [category] that [does what others can't] — [specific proof].`
**Example:**
> The creative agency enterprises call when every other agency says two weeks.

---

### Formula 6 — The Scale Signal
**When to use:** About page, team sections, trust blocks.
**Pattern:** `[Number] [asset]. [Number] [asset]. [Number] [asset]. [Time context].`
**Example:**
> 35 experts. 5 continents. 18 languages. 24/7.

---

### Formula 7 — The Guarantee Headline
**When to use:** Risk-reversal blocks, pricing page footer, proposal cover pages.
**Pattern:** `Not satisfied with your first project? [Consequence that removes all risk].`
**Example:**
> Not satisfied with your first project? No invoice.

## 6. Do / Don't Examples

Ten pairs drawn from real Sarani contexts. Each pair shows the same intent executed in Sarani voice (DO) versus a common agency default (DON'T). Use these to calibrate any writer, AI pipeline, or content tool.

---

**1. Introducing the agency on the homepage**

DO: "The creative agency enterprises call when every other agency says two weeks."
DON'T: "A leading full-service integrated creative solutions provider empowering global brands."

*Why the DO works: It names a specific frustration (two weeks), implies the competition implicitly, and positions Sarani without a single adjective.*

---

**2. Describing delivery speed**

DO: "Sony's Black Friday banners. Ordered in the morning. Delivered the same day."
DON'T: "We offer industry-leading turnaround times to meet your most urgent deadlines."

*Why the DO works: It is a scene, not a claim. The reader visualises the situation. "Industry-leading" is empty; "delivered the same day" is a fact.*

---

**3. Addressing price**

DO: "A simple banner starts at 155€. Full rebranding: 5,000€. No hidden fees."
DON'T: "Our competitive rates are tailored to your project scope and budget."

*Why the DO works: Three numbers in two sentences. Marc can benchmark this against any competitor. "Tailored to your budget" is a red flag for procurement — it means the price is negotiable downward AND upward.*

---

**4. Explaining the guarantee**

DO: "Not satisfied with your first project? No invoice."
DON'T: "We stand behind our work and are committed to your complete satisfaction."

*Why the DO works: Seven words. The consequence is concrete and immediate. "Committed to your satisfaction" is what every agency says. "No invoice" is what no agency says.*

---

**5. Describing the team**

DO: "35 experts across 5 continents, working in time-zone relay so your brief never waits for a timezone to wake up."
DON'T: "Our talented and diverse global team is always available to support your creative needs."

*Why the DO works: The relay mechanism explains HOW the 24/7 is structurally possible. "Always available" is a claim. Explaining the relay is proof.*

---

**6. Responding to the "can you handle our volume?" objection**

DO: "TikTok sends us 300–500 videos a week. We deliver 1,500+ edits a month. What's your volume?"
DON'T: "We have the capacity and scalability to handle projects of any size."

*Why the DO works: It answers with a number, names the client, and immediately turns the question back into a brief. "Scalability" is jargon that adds nothing.*

---

**7. Confirming a project submission (success message)**

DO: "Got it. Expect a response within the hour — usually faster."
DON'T: "Thank you for your submission. Our team will review your inquiry and respond within 2–3 business days."

*Why the DO works: It commits to a specific timeframe (one hour). "Usually faster" adds warmth and credibility. "2–3 business days" would immediately disqualify Sarani with Sophie.*

---

**8. Writing a LinkedIn post about a case study**

DO: "Adidas needed 92 assets for their marathon booth. We turned them around before the event. This is what that looked like."
DON'T: "Excited to share that we had the amazing opportunity to work with Adidas on their incredible marathon campaign! 🎉"

*Why the DO works: No emotion, no emoji, no self-congratulation. The case study proves itself. Sophie's feed is full of brands celebrating themselves — a clean, factual post stands out.*

---

**9. Explaining the no-subscription model**

DO: "No retainer. No minimum commitment. You pay per project, benchmark quality and price each time, and scale when you need to."
DON'T: "We offer flexible engagement models to suit your business requirements."

*Why the DO works: It directly addresses Marc's core anxiety (lock-in). Three sentences explain the commercial logic. "Flexible engagement models" is a phrase that means literally anything.*

---

**10. Describing a price advantage**

DO: "Up to 60% savings vs traditional agencies — because our structure is built differently, not because we cut corners."
DON'T: "We provide premium quality at a fraction of the cost of traditional agencies."

*Why the DO works: The second clause ("because our structure is built differently, not because we cut corners") preemptively answers the quality objection. It shows Sarani has anticipated Sophie's internal monologue. "Fraction of the cost" is a cliché that triggers the cheap-quality association.*

## 9. Message Variants from Messaging Matrix

Six priority cases from the messaging matrix. Each case has 2 distinct variants — different angle, not just reformulation — with a recommendation and rationale.

---

#### 9.1 — Sophie × Landing Page × Discovery (Hero)

**Variant A — Evidence-first (Formula 1):**
- H1: Every other agency said two weeks. We said tomorrow.
- H2: TikTok trusts us with 1,500+ video edits a month. Sony gets Black Friday banners same-day for 155€. Enterprise-quality creative. Fixed prices. No retainer.

**Variant B — Scale + speed signal (Formula 6 → 5):**
- H1: Enterprise creative. 24 hours. TikTok, Sony, Adidas.
- H2: GEODIS rebranded 350 presentations in 3 weeks for 8,500€. Their previous agency quoted 80,000€ and 3 months. Fixed prices. Unlimited revisions. First project satisfaction or no invoice.

*Recommended: B — because the H1 leads with three named clients immediately, removing the 3-second credibility gap. Variant A is stronger for retargeting (where Sophie already knows Sarani), but weaker as a cold first impression.*

---

#### 9.2 — Sophie × Email × Discovery (Cold outreach)

**Variant A — Named client anchor (TikTok volume):**
- Subject: How TikTok gets 1,500 videos edited a month
- Opening: Your agency just quoted two weeks. TikTok sends us 300–500 videos a week — we return 1,500+ edits a month, on time, every month.

**Variant B — Cost shock anchor (GEODIS comparison):**
