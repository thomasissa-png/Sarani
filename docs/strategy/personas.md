## Persona 1 — Sophie

**Role:** Head of Marketing / Directrice Communication, large international enterprise
**Age:** 38
**Location:** Paris, London, or Dubai (global hub cities)
**Company type:** Global enterprise (TikTok, Sony, Adidas, L'Oréal, Pernod Ricard, GEODIS profile)
**Budget managed:** 500K€ to 5M€/year in communications and marketing
**Reports to:** CMO or CEO
**Priority level for Sarani:** PRIMARY — she is the person who initiates the brief, feels the pain daily, and champions the vendor internally

---

### Context & Environment

Sophie runs the marketing engine of a global enterprise. Her week is a sequence of competing deadlines: a campaign launching in 3 markets simultaneously, a board deck due Monday, a Black Friday asset set needed by Thursday (which is today), a TikTok video series for next week. She has an internal team but it is never large enough. She works with agencies but they always disappoint — either on speed, price, or communication.

She has been burned before. The agency that quoted 3 weeks for a banner refresh. The one that sent a new account manager every 6 months. The one that charged 800€ for a revision that took 15 minutes. She now pre-qualifies every new agency with the same invisible test: "Will you be there when I actually need you?"

---

### Jobs-to-be-done (JTBD)

1. **Speed JTBD:** "Get campaign assets live before the window closes — even if the brief came in this morning."
2. **Volume JTBD:** "Produce 300–1,500 assets per month without building a production studio in-house."
3. **Quality JTBD:** "Maintain brand consistency across 15 languages and 5 markets without a local agency in each country."
4. **Budget JTBD:** "Justify creative spend to my CFO with a clear price-per-asset, not a black-box retainer."
5. **Reliability JTBD:** "Have a partner I can trust to never miss a deadline — even if I send the brief at 11pm."

2. **"What's the quality level — are these junior designers?"**
   *Answer:* Sony trusted Sarani with a 125-asset TV launch. L'Oréal, Adidas, Pernod Ricard — these clients have the highest brand standards in the world.

3. **"D+1 is a marketing claim — what's the reality?"**
   *Answer:* Sony Black Friday banners: same-day delivery. It is the standard, not the exception.

4. **"I've worked with cheap agencies before and the quality was terrible."**
   *Answer:* Sarani is not cheap. It is priced fairly. The 60% savings vs. traditional agencies comes from operational efficiency, not quality reduction. The client roster is the proof.

5. **"We have preferred vendor lists and procurement processes."**
   *Answer:* Marc (Procurement) is a secondary persona specifically addressed — see Persona 2.

## Persona 2 — Marc

**Role:** Directeur Achats / Procurement Director, large international enterprise
**Age:** 45
**Location:** Paris, Frankfurt, London (headquarters of global enterprise groups)
**Company type:** Enterprise with revenue >500M€ (same profile as Sophie's employer)
**Budget managed:** Responsible for validating and renewing vendor contracts, including creative agency spend
**Reports to:** CFO or COO
**Priority level for Sarani:** SECONDARY — Marc is the gatekeeper. Sophie creates the demand; Marc approves or blocks the vendor. Sarani needs to satisfy both.

---

### Context & Environment

Marc did not initiate the relationship with Sarani. Sophie did. Marc's job is to validate that Sophie's choice meets procurement standards: is the pricing fair? Is the contract structured properly? Is this vendor reliable enough to put on the approved vendor list? Will this relationship create dependency risk?

He evaluates vendors primarily on: price transparency, proof of delivery, compliance, and contract terms. He is not excited by creative work — he is excited by predictable costs and measurable performance.

His core tension: he wants to reduce spend and simplify vendor lists (fewer, better suppliers). When Sophie comes to him with Sarani, he evaluates whether Sarani can replace, or at least complement, a more expensive incumbent.

---

4. **Simplification JTBD:** "If this agency can replace 3 local agencies, that's a win for me."

## Persona Priority & Decision Dynamics

**Sophie triggers the relationship. Marc validates it. Both must be satisfied to close.**

The sales motion typically unfolds in this order:
1. Sophie has a crisis brief or capacity problem → searches for an agency → hears about Sarani from a colleague (referral) or finds it via site / LinkedIn
2. Sophie sends a first brief — Sarani delivers, impresses, removes friction
3. Sophie becomes an advocate internally and brings Sarani to Marc
4. Marc evaluates the vendor on cost/risk/compliance criteria
5. If Marc approves → framework agreement or recurring project flow → Sarani becomes a strategic vendor

**Implication for messaging:**
- The **website and marketing content** should speak primarily to Sophie (speed, quality, named clients, easy onboarding)
- The **commercial deck and vendor proposals** should speak to Marc (pricing tables, reference accounts, contract terms, savings metrics)
- The **guarantee** ("first project or no invoice") serves both: it lowers Sophie's emotional risk AND Marc's financial risk

**Multi-persona communication rule:** Never try to address both in the same sentence. Sophie and Marc read differently. One page for each.
