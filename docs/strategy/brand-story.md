## 1. Origin Story

**The year was 2020. The world had just stopped.**

Every agency in the world was scrambling to cut costs, freeze hiring, and survive. Sarani was being built.

Not despite the pandemic — in some ways, because of it. The crisis made one thing impossible to ignore: the traditional agency model was already broken before COVID. The lockdowns did not create the problem. They made it undeniable.

Large enterprises still needed to communicate. Their campaigns did not pause. Their boards still expected assets on Monday morning. But the agencies they relied on were built for a world that no longer existed — hierarchical structures, single-timezone teams, weekly status meetings, and billing models that made every revision a negotiation.

In 2020, Thomas and the founding team of Sarani made a decision that ran counter to every industry norm:

Instead of building a local studio, they built a distributed team across 5 continents. Instead of one timezone, they designed for 24/7 relay production. Instead of opaque retainer pricing, they published fixed rates. Instead of treating revisions as a revenue line, they made them unlimited.

The idea was not to be a cheaper agency. The idea was to be a structurally different one.

By 2026, Sarani had delivered 1,500+ monthly video edits for TikTok. Sony's Black Friday banners, ordered and delivered same day. GEODIS's entire presentation deck — 350 rebranded files, 5,700 slides — in 3 weeks, for 8,500€. Without a single subscription lock-in.

That is what 45 experts across 5 continents, working in relay, can do. Not occasionally. Every week.

## 2. Narrative Tension

**What the market does that is no longer acceptable.**

Sophie is Head of Marketing at a global enterprise. She knows exactly what she needs. She has the brief, the budget, and the deadline. What she cannot find — consistently, reliably, at scale — is an agency that treats her deadline as the starting point, not a negotiating position.

Here is what the rest of the market offers:

- **Two weeks for a banner.** A revision triggers a scope change request. The account manager who understood the brief has been replaced. The quote that was approved in Q3 is now "subject to recalibration."
- **$10,000/month minimum** before a single asset is produced (Superside). A 12-month commitment signed before the relationship is proven.
- **Separate agencies** for each language market. Three briefing processes. Three invoices. Three interpretations of the brand guidelines.
- **Opaque pricing.** Day rates. Hourly estimates that expand with every revision. Budgets that bear no relationship to the final invoice.

This is not a failure of individual agencies. It is a structural outcome of how traditional agencies were built — for a world where weekly client meetings were standard, where campaigns ran for quarters not days, where "fast" meant two weeks instead of four.

**What Sarani refuses.**

Sarani was built to refuse every one of these compromises — not as a policy, but as a structural commitment:

- No revision surcharges. Not as a concession. As a design principle.
- No subscription lock-in. A client relationship should be earned project by project, not locked in by contract.
- No opaque pricing. Rates are published on the website. A banner starts at 155€. A full rebranding is 5,000€. What is quoted is what is billed.
- No single timezone. When Sophie's brief arrives at 6pm Paris time, her team in Asia has already started. The work is done before she wakes up.
- No "best effort" on deadlines. D+1 is the default — not a premium add-on, not a special arrangement for key clients.

**The rupture incarnated.**

The Sony team needed Black Friday banners. Not tomorrow. That day. Every agency they called said the same thing: "We'll need a brief, a scope review, and we can turn it around in 5–7 business days."

Sarani delivered the same day. 155€ per banner.

That is not a case study. That is a proof of concept for a different way of working — one that TikTok, Sony, Adidas, GEODIS, and L'Oréal have all validated, repeatedly, with real money and real campaigns.

## 3. Vision 3–5 Years

**Where Sarani is going — and why it matters beyond Sarani.**

The immediate ambition is specific: 10M€ revenue at 20% EBITDA. Five new enterprise accounts per month. A presence in every major marketing conversation when global enterprises look for a creative production partner who can actually move at the speed of their campaigns.

But the larger vision is not about Sarani's growth curve. It is about what a different model proves possible.

**Enterprise-grade creativity should not require an enterprise-sized commitment.**

Today, the structural reality for any Head of Marketing at a 500M€+ company is a binary choice: pay premium subscription rates for reliability ($10,000/month, 12-month lock-in), or accept the chaos of traditional agency timelines. Sarani exists to eliminate that choice.

By 2030, the goal is to make "D+1 delivery with unlimited revisions and fixed pricing" not a differentiator — but the standard that every enterprise creative partner is measured against. Sarani pioneering this model at scale, with named enterprise clients across TikTok, Sony, Adidas, GEODIS, L'Oréal, and Pernod Ricard, is the proof that it works.

**The concrete milestones:**

- 4M€ revenue at 6 months (current priority: the site as the primary conversion engine)
- 10M€ revenue at 20% EBITDA (North Star: sustainable, profitable growth — not venture-backed hypergrowth)
- 5 new enterprise accounts per month (>500M€ revenue), sustained
- Sarani recognized as the reference creative production partner for international enterprises in Paris, London, and Dubai — the three cities where Sophie works

**What this unlocks for clients.**

A world where global enterprises do not have to choose between quality, speed, and price. Where a Head of Marketing can brief a campaign on Monday and present final assets on Tuesday — in 18 languages, for 5 markets, without touching five different agencies. Where the budget for creative production reflects the work itself, not the agency's overhead structure.

That is Sarani's operating vision. Not aspirational. Already partly real. TikTok and Sony are proof it works. The next five years are about doing it at 10x the scale.

## 4. Manifesto

**We refuse to accept that fast and good are a trade-off.**

For too long, enterprise marketing teams have been told to choose: move fast, or move well. Brief an agency, wait two weeks, receive a revision invoice. Sign a $10,000/month subscription before you've seen a single asset. Manage five agencies for five language markets. Accept that the account manager who understood your brand last month has been replaced.

Sarani was built to prove that this is not a law of nature. It is a legacy of how agencies were structured — and structure can be changed.

We built a team of 45 experts across 5 continents because time zones should work for our clients, not against them. When your brief arrives at 6pm Paris time, your work has already started. When you wake up, it is done.

We published our prices — not because we had to, but because transparency is not a strategy. It is a minimum standard. A banner starts at 155€. A rebranding is 5,000€. What is quoted is what is billed. Every time.

We made revisions unlimited because a client who cannot ask for a correction without counting the cost is not a partner. They are a hostage.

TikTok trusted us with 1,500 video edits a month. Sony called us the day their Black Friday banners were needed — not the day before, that day. GEODIS gave us 350 presentations to rebrand in 3 weeks. We delivered every one.

This is not for everyone. If you need strategic brand discovery, a boardroom-friendly agency name, or a monthly retainer to feel secure — there are agencies built for that. We are not one of them.

We are for the Head of Marketing who already knows what she wants, has the brief in hand, and cannot afford to wait. We are for the Procurement Director who needs a partner that delivers on time, at the quoted price, without the procurement headache of annual commitment risk.

We are the creative agency enterprises call when every other agency says two weeks.

We say: tomorrow.
